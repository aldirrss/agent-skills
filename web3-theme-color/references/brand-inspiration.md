# Brand Inspiration — Why These Three?

Web3 brands have converged on a recognizable visual language. This document explains which crypto/blockchain brand colors translate well to general product design — and which do not.

## Picked

### Solana — `#9945FF` → `#14F195`
The signature gradient. More than any other Web3 brand, Solana's purple-to-mint gradient has become shorthand for "this is a crypto product." Used unmodified or as inspiration, it signals Web3 instantly.

### Polygon — `#8247E5`
A single violet that reads premium without being aggressive. Easy to extend into a full color scale.

### Uniswap — `#FF007A`
Anti-mainstream. While most Web3 apps reach for blue or purple, Uniswap's hot pink stands out and gives products personality.

## Considered but Not Picked

### Ethereum — `#627EEA`
Too close to corporate blue (legacy Facebook, Twitter, LinkedIn). Lacks the "Web3 feel" — users won't read it as crypto. Best used **only** when you specifically want to associate with Ethereum.

### Coinbase — `#0052FF`
Generic, saturated blue. Designed to communicate trust like a traditional bank — which is the opposite of the Web3 aesthetic. Skip unless building a CEX clone.

### Binance — `#F0B90B`
Yellow is one of the hardest colors to use in UI: poor contrast against white, terrible as text color, struggles to feel premium. Use only as a small accent (badges, highlight chips), never as primary.

### OpenSea — `#2081E2`
Beautiful color, but so identified with OpenSea that any app using it will look derivative. Their blue + their gradient pattern is essentially trademarked-by-association.

### Arbitrum — `#28A0F0`
Same problem as OpenSea: too brand-specific. Cyan-blue is also widely overused in tech.

## Color Theory Notes

**Why purple dominates Web3**: Historically, finance was blue (trust). Web3 wanted to differentiate from TradFi while still feeling "tech." Purple — between blue (tech) and red (energy) — became the natural choice. It also has cyberpunk/futurist associations from sci-fi.

**Why neon greens and pinks work**: Web3 inherits visual DNA from gaming and cyberpunk aesthetics. Neon colors evoke screens, code, and digital-native culture. They feel "online" in a way muted earth tones do not.

**Why gold appears constantly**: Crypto = "digital gold" rhetoric. Gold accents on Web3 UIs reference wealth, scarcity, and value storage. Use sparingly for premium tiers, rewards, achievements.

**Why dark mode dominates**: OLED-friendly, easier on eyes during long trading sessions, makes neon accents pop. Most Web3 users default to dark.
